In 1777 [Gilbert White](#per_wh1te0), the curate of Selborne, wrote to a friend that earthworms, small as they seem, would leave "a lamentable chasm" in nature if they were lost. Few naturalists of his day agreed. A worm was a pest of lawns and a bait for fish.

White's letters were printed in [The Natural History of Selborne](#src_se1b0r) in 1789, and the book was read for a century. Its point about worms waited longer: that the dark upper layer of a field, its [vegetable mould](#con_m0vd00), might be their work.
